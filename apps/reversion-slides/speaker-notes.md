# Speaker notes

22 slides, about 12 to 15 minutes at a calm pace. The deck is
`index.html`; press N in the deck to see these on screen while presenting.

Film cut points refer to **apps/reversion-film** playing with narration, total
running time 14:13. Timecodes are from the narrated build, not the
authored one, so they will be wrong if the narration is ever re-synthesised at a
different speed.


## 01 · Reversion to the Mean

Open cold. Do not explain what regression to the mean is yet. Say the title, then go straight to a dead man in 1877.

## 02 · The richest man in America died.

Let the number sit. $105m in 1877 is not a number anyone has intuition for, so the households figure is doing the work: he is one household in nine and a half million.

## 03 · The family walked back into the crowd.

**Roll film 0:54** — the staircase building, one generation at a time

Trace it left with your hand as you talk. The Commodore, his son who nearly doubled it, then the split eight ways, then the trust spent on horses, then Gloria's estate under one and a half million.

## 04 · A hundred and twenty relatives. Not one millionaire.

This is the hook landing. Pause after 'not one millionaire'.

## 05 · Spending. Marble houses, yachts, a family that stopped building and started decorating.

Concede the obvious explanation properly before taking it away. If you rush this, the rest sounds like a trick.

## 06 · A man set out to prove that greatness breeds greatness.

Worth saying plainly that Galton's motive was eugenic and that his own data refused him. That refusal is the whole reason this is trustworthy.

## 07 · His own numbers refused him.

**Roll film 2:54** — the dots raining in, then both lines drawing

Point at the dashed line first: that is the world people carry in their heads, children match parents. Then the solid one. Then the two places they separate, far left and far right.

## 08 · A child kept about two thirds of the parents' distance from average, and handed the rest back.

**Roll film 3:07** — the counter running from 1.00 down

The single most important number in the talk. Say it slowly. Everything after this is a consequence of it.

## 09 · He named the finding after his own disappointment.

The name is why people misunderstand this. 'Regression' sounds like a force. It is not a force, it is arithmetic.

## 10 · One multiplication.

Write it in the air if you have to. The zero-times-anything point is what makes it click for most people.

## 11 · An equation from 1886 missed a living person by four tenths of an inch.

**Roll film 5:01** — the multiplication travelling down the ruler

The instrument detail matters: same combine, same measurement, twenty one years apart. That is what makes it a fair test rather than a coincidence.

## 12 · It predicts a crowd, not a person.

Do not skip this. Saying the limits out loud is what stops the whole talk sounding like astrology.

## 13 · A box with pegs in it.

**Roll film 6:06** — 232 balls falling, the bell forming

Roll the film here rather than describing it. The board moving does more in eight seconds than a slide can.

## 14 · Keep only the far right balls. Drop them again.

**Roll film 6:17** — the far right selected, then re-dropped

This is the mechanism, and it is the moment the talk either lands or does not. Nothing pushes them back.

## 15 · An extreme number is a durable part plus a lucky part. Only one of them has children.

Land this flat. It is the thesis of the whole piece and it does not need a flourish.

## 16 · A quarterback, a prime minister and a department store fortune do not share a unit. So we gave them one.

**Roll film 7:57** — forty rows assembling

Open mark is the parent, filled mark is the child. Sorted by how far out the parent stood. Say that the sample is famous families and therefore biased; you pay that debt two slides later.

## 17 · A generation hands back about 38 per cent of whatever the parent was above the mean.

**Roll film 8:12** — the slope counter

Then immediately: 20 fell, 13 rose, 7 came out exactly level. It is a tilt, not a law. Stephen Curry rose and did not break anything: regression runs toward the mean, not downward, and Dell Curry had a long way above him to go.

## 18 · The more transferable the advantage, the slower it fades.

**Roll film 9:43** — the columns decaying

Height halves in 0.9 generations, surname status in 2.4. That gap is why we can name every sporting dynasty but political families run for centuries.

## 19 · Almost half the children never become measurable at all.

**Roll film 11:30** — the invisible children appearing

This is the intellectual honesty slide. The chart anyone can draw is a survivor's chart, including ours.

## 20 · Kahneman's flight instructors had years of evidence.

The best story in the talk. Those instructors were not fools; they were doing what everybody does. We are built to find the cause of a change, and a change with no cause in it is nearly impossible to hold in your head.

## 21 · Less extreme is not the same as ordinary.

End on the last line and stop. Do not add a summary. 'What the arithmetic knew about him, it knew before he was born. It never knew very much.'

## 22 · Everything here is checkable.

Point people at the film and the written version. Say plainly that the forty families are a biased convenience sample and that the survivor slide is why.
